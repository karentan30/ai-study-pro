NGN Clinical Scenario Cardiac · Priority Action
A nurse is caring for a 68-year-old male admitted for an acute inferior wall MI. He received thrombolytics 2 hours ago. Current vitals: BP 88/54 mmHg, HR 112 bpm (irregular), RR 22/min, SpO₂ 91% on 2L NC. He reports chest pressure 6/10 and new onset of confusion. His 12-lead ECG shows new ST elevation in leads II, III, and aVF with reciprocal changes in I and aVL. Urine output for the last hour is 18 mL.

The nurse notes the patient is diaphoretic and his skin is cool and mottled from the knees down.

Which nursing intervention should the nurse implement FIRST?

A Administer a 500 mL NS bolus IV to treat hypotension
B Increase supplemental oxygen and reposition the patient in Trendelenburg
C Notify the provider immediately and prepare for emergent cardiac catheterization
D Obtain a repeat 12-lead ECG and reassess in 15 minutes

✓ Correct Answer: C — Notify provider & prepare for cath lab

Why C is correct: This patient is in cardiogenic shock following acute inferior wall MI — evidenced by hypotension (BP 88/54), tachycardia, decreased urine output (18 mL/hr → oliguria), confusion (cerebral hypoperfusion), cool mottled extremities, and SpO₂ decline. Despite receiving thrombolytics, the persistent ST elevation indicates likely failed reperfusion. The priority is emergent percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), which requires immediate provider notification. Time-to-balloon is the most critical outcome determinant in STEMI.

Why A is wrong: A fluid bolus is contraindicated in cardiogenic shock. The left ventricle is already failing — adding volume increases preload and worsens pulmonary edema without improving cardiac output.

Why B is wrong: Trendelenburg is no longer recommended for shock and is contraindicated with acute MI (increases venous return to a failing heart). Supplemental O₂ adjustment is secondary to the emergent need for revascularization.

Why D is wrong: Reassessing in 15 minutes wastes critical time. This patient meets STEMI criteria and shows evidence of cardiogenic shock — delay directly increases myocardial necrosis and mortality. Time is muscle.

NCLEX Tip: When a post-MI patient develops hypotension + decreased urine output + altered mental status, think cardiogenic shock → priority = revascularization, not fluid resuscitation.

The Next Generation NCLEX (NGN) launched in April 2023 and introduced new question types designed to test clinical judgment, not just recall. NGN formats include: case studies (a 6-item set based on one patient scenario), extended multiple-response, extended drag-and-drop, cloze (drop-down), enhanced hot spot (text highlighting), and matrix/grid questions. These require nurses to demonstrate the Clinical Judgment Measurement Model (CJMM) — the same cognitive process used in real clinical settings.
